PhD candidate

Jules Ferreira

I study the macroevolution of beetles (Coleoptera), the most species-rich group of living organisms, combining their fossil record with phylogenies to understand how they diversified through time.

I am a macroevolutionist, taxonomist and systematist working on Coleoptera, currently completing my PhD under the supervision of Dr David Peris (Institut Botànic de Barcelona) and Dr Fabien L. Condamine (Institut des Sciences de l’Évolution de Montpellier). Working at the interface between neontology and palaeontology, I describe new fossil and extant beetle taxa and analyse phylogenies and fossil data, separately or together, to date clades and try to understand how beetle lineages diversified. To do so, I employ state-of-the-art phylogenetic and macroevolutionary methods.

New paper · November 2026

Where did diving beetles come from, and what shapes their range size?

We reconstructed the historical biogeography of diving beetles (Dytiscidae), which probably originated in the Nearctic during the Late Triassic (c. 210 Ma), and found that habitat type, niche breadth and diversification rates are associated with their range size, whereas body size is not.

New paper · November 2026

First fossil puppet beetle described from the 53-million-year-old Oise amber

†Cnopus twentyonepilotorum Ferreira & Nel, 2026 is the first puppet beetle (Aderidae) formally described from the lowermost Eocene Oise amber of France, studied with synchrotron X-ray microtomography. Its name honours the band Twenty One Pilots.

Research

Fossil beetles

Description of fossil Coleoptera, with a particular focus on amber inclusions, from mid-Cretaceous to Eocene deposits, using light microscopy and, when needed or available, X-ray microtomography and 3D reconstructions.

Beetle macroevolution

Macroevolution of Coleoptera at a broad scale, combining phylogenies and the fossil record in Bayesian frameworks to estimate divergence times and diversification dynamics.

Macroevolution of specific lineages in Coleoptera

Focused studies of particular beetle lineages: dating clades, reconstructing their historical biogeography and exploring the mechanisms potentially linked to their diversification, such as competition, traits, habitats and biogeographic range size.

Beyond the lab

Insects are my passion, and beetles my favourite obsession. I love searching for them in the field, and museum collections are an essential part of my daily work. What still fascinates me most is how amber can preserve fossils in such exquisite detail that, despite their great age, they look as if they had been trapped yesterday. Each specimen, living or fossil, is a small window onto millions of years of evolution. And when I am not looking at beetles, there is a good chance I am listening to rock or metal.

Science meets music · November 2026

†Cnopus twentyonepilotorum, a 53-million-year-old beetle named after Twenty One Pilots

A puppet beetle (Aderidae) the size of a poppy seed (1.09 mm), preserved in the lowermost Eocene Oise amber of France and studied with synchrotron X-ray microtomography. Its name honours the band Twenty One Pilots for helping to make mental health easier to talk about. The story reached science news sites, music media and the band’s fan community, bringing a fossil beetle to an audience that rarely hears about palaeontology.

Science meets music · November 2026

†Imaginedraconium daemon: a 53-million-year-old beetle in honour of Imagine Dragons

A tiny Ptinidae beetle from the lowermost Eocene Oise amber of France, the first fossil of the “Cryptoramorphini” group, which today lives mainly in tropical and subtropical regions. Synchrotron X-ray microtomography at ALBA revealed its underside, hidden under the optical microscope. Its name honours the band Imagine Dragons for their support of environmental sustainability and climate action.

Ferreira, J., Spiessberger, E. L., Nel, A., Patera, A., & Peris, D. (2026). Annals of the Entomological Society of America, saag043 · Read the article ↗ · ALBA Synchrotron news ↗
